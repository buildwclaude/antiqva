From the editors

Nothing here is standing still

A monument is built to refuse the day. It is quarried, squared and set above the street precisely so that nothing in it will move again. And yet the first thing any painter does with a monument is give it weather — a wind from the left, a hem caught mid-lift, a bolt of crimson going out of the frame like a sentence that refuses its own full stop.

The frontispiece of this issue is a case in point. The figure is stone; the cloth is not. Between the two the painter has hidden an argument about permanence that the nineteenth century never quite settled, and that we have spent fourteen issues failing to settle either.

Our contributors take the question from three directions: the workshop, the archive and the street. Mariana Oreste reads the fluting of the column as a record of labour rather than of order. Jan Verhoeven follows a single pigment — a madder lake, unstable, gorgeous — from Amsterdam to Rome and back.

“Drapery is the part of a statue that has agreed to be mortal.”

— M. Oreste, On Fluting

What follows are six plates, taken at close range from the frontispiece. Read them as a printer would: slowly, from the edges inward, with the light behind you.
